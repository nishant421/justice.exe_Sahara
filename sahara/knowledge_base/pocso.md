# POCSO Act 2012 — Protection of Children from Sexual Offences

## Overview

The Protection of Children from Sexual Offences (POCSO) Act 2012 is the primary Indian law protecting children under the age of 18 from sexual abuse, harassment, and exploitation. The Act is gender-neutral — it protects all children regardless of gender. It establishes Special Courts for speedy trial, mandates child-friendly procedures, and creates a mandatory reporting obligation on every citizen. POCSO overrides any other law where it conflicts, ensuring maximum protection for children.

## Who is a Child Under POCSO

Under POCSO, a "child" means any person below the age of 18 years. Age is the sole criterion — marital status, past conduct, or relationship to the accused does not affect this definition. A child cannot legally consent to any sexual act. The Supreme Court in Independent Thought v Union of India (2017) held that even a married girl below 18 is fully protected under POCSO and the marital exemption does not apply.

## Section 3 and 4 — Penetrative Sexual Assault

Section 3 defines Penetrative Sexual Assault as any act of penetration of a child's vagina, mouth, urethra or anus, or making the child do so, or any application of a body part or object. Section 4 prescribes punishment: rigorous imprisonment for not less than 10 years, extendable to imprisonment for life, plus fine. The minimum sentence is mandatory — courts cannot award less.

## Section 5 and 6 — Aggravated Penetrative Sexual Assault

Section 5 defines aggravated penetrative sexual assault — cases where the offender is a police officer, public servant, armed forces member, management or staff of a jail, remand home, hospital, educational institution, religious institution, or a relative of the child. Also aggravated when the child is below 12 years, when the act causes grievous hurt, mental illness, or pregnancy. Section 6 prescribes punishment: rigorous imprisonment of not less than 20 years, extendable to imprisonment for life (meaning the remainder of natural life) or death penalty.

## Section 7 and 8 — Sexual Assault

Section 7 defines Sexual Assault as touching a child's vagina, penis, anus or breast with sexual intent, or making the child touch the offender's body parts, or any other act of physical contact with sexual intent. Section 8 prescribes punishment: rigorous imprisonment of 3 to 5 years plus fine. Aggravated sexual assault (Section 9-10) by persons in positions of authority carries 5 to 7 years imprisonment.

## Section 11 and 12 — Sexual Harassment

Section 11 defines Sexual Harassment of a child as: making sexually colored remarks, repeatedly following or watching, exposing genitals, flashing, any act done electronically or using media. Section 12 prescribes punishment: imprisonment up to 3 years plus fine. This section covers online grooming and cyber harassment of children.

## Section 13, 14, 15 — Child Pornography

Section 13 prohibits using a child for pornographic purposes. Section 14 prescribes punishment of up to 5 years (and up to 7 years for repeat offence). Section 15 prohibits storing child pornography — punishment up to 3 years or fine or both. Even possessing or viewing child sexual abuse material is a criminal offence under POCSO.

## Section 19 — Mandatory Reporting Obligation

Section 19 is one of the most important provisions of POCSO. It creates a mandatory duty on EVERY PERSON who apprehends that a sexual offence has been or is likely to be committed against a child to report it to the Special Juvenile Police Unit (SJPU) or local police. This includes parents, teachers, doctors, neighbours, friends — anyone. Failure to report is itself a criminal offence. The report can be made verbally or in writing. Police must record it immediately and cannot refuse. The reporter does not need to have proof — a reasonable apprehension is sufficient. Reports can also be made by calling Childline 1098.

## Section 21 — Punishment for Failure to Report

Section 21 makes failure to report a POCSO offence punishable with imprisonment up to 6 months or fine or both. Persons in charge of institutions (schools, hospitals, shelter homes) who fail to report face enhanced punishment. Media persons who disclose a child victim's identity face fine up to Rs. 1 lakh for first offence and imprisonment plus fine for repeat offence.

## Section 22 — False Complaint Protection

Section 22 protects accused persons from malicious prosecution. Making a false complaint against someone knowing it to be false is punishable with 6 months imprisonment or fine. However, a child making a false complaint in good faith is not punishable. This section does not discourage genuine reporting — it only targets deliberate false accusations.

## Section 24 — Recording Child's Statement by Police

Under Section 24, a child's statement must be recorded at the child's residence or a place of the child's choice — NOT at the police station. The police officer must be in plain clothes (not uniform). The statement must be recorded in the presence of the child's parent or a person the child trusts. A woman police officer must record the statement if the victim is a girl. The child cannot be made to stay overnight at the police station under any circumstances.

## Section 25 — Recording Statement by Magistrate

Under Section 25, a Magistrate must record the child's statement in a manner that does not cause trauma. The child should not see the accused. The Magistrate may use a single-way mirror, screen, or audio-video facilities to protect the child. A Special Educator or qualified interpreter must assist if needed. The statement should ideally be video-recorded.

## Section 26 — Safeguards During Recording

Section 26 ensures: the child is not repeatedly questioned; the statement may be video-graphed; the child shall not be exposed to the accused at any stage; the child's identity shall be protected. Medical examination of the child must be conducted by a female doctor and in the presence of the child's parent or trusted person.

## Section 27 — Medical Examination

Under Section 27, medical examination of a child victim must be conducted by a doctor. For girl children, the examination must be conducted by a female doctor. The examination must be in the presence of a parent or a person the child trusts. The child's consent must be taken where the child is capable of giving it. No two-finger test or invasive examination procedure that violates the child's dignity is permitted.

## Section 28 — Special Courts

Section 28 mandates that every State Government designate Special Courts for speedy trial of POCSO offences. Special Courts are presided over by a judge with experience and sensitivity in dealing with child matters. The trial is conducted in camera (privately) to protect the child's identity and dignity. The Special Court may allow a support person to accompany the child during testimony.

## Section 29 and 30 — Presumption of Guilt

Sections 29 and 30 create a reversed burden of proof for certain POCSO offences. Once the prosecution establishes that the accused had committed the act, the court shall presume the accused guilty unless they prove their innocence. This protection recognizes the severe power imbalance between the accused and the child victim.

## Section 39 and 40 — Child-Friendly Procedures

Section 39 requires the National Commission for Protection of Child Rights (NCPCR) to develop guidelines for child-friendly procedures. Section 40 ensures the trial is conducted in a child-friendly atmosphere. The child may be allowed to give testimony via video-link to avoid facing the accused. A support person (social worker, counsellor, parent) may accompany the child throughout the process.

## Reporting a POCSO Offence — Step-by-Step

Step 1: Report to the nearest police station or call Childline 1098 (free, 24x7). Step 2: Police must register an FIR — they cannot refuse. If police refuse, complain to the District Superintendent of Police. Step 3: Police will contact the Special Juvenile Police Unit (SJPU). Step 4: Child's statement will be recorded at home by a woman officer in plain clothes. Step 5: Medical examination will be conducted by a female doctor. Step 6: The case will be heard in a Special Court. Step 7: Legal aid is available free of charge through NALSA (call 15100).

## Compensation for Child Victims

Under Section 33(8) and 357A CrPC, the Special Court can direct the State to pay compensation to the child victim regardless of whether the accused is convicted. Compensation covers medical expenses, loss of educational opportunity, and emotional trauma. The National Legal Services Authority (NALSA) manages the victim compensation fund. Legal aid lawyers will assist in claiming compensation.

## Confidentiality and Identity Protection

Under Section 23, no media outlet, social media user, or individual may disclose any information that could identify a child victim of a POCSO offence. The child's name, school, locality, photograph, family details — all are protected. Violation is a criminal offence. The Special Court's judgment must also not contain identifying information. Even the FIR should use the child's initials, not full name.
