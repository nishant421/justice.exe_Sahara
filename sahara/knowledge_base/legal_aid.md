# Legal Aid in India — Access to Justice

## Overview

Every person in India has the constitutional right to free legal aid under Article 39A of the Constitution. This right is implemented through the Legal Services Authorities Act 1987, which established the National Legal Services Authority (NALSA) at the national level, State Legal Services Authorities (SLSAs) at the state level, and District Legal Services Authorities (DLSAs) at the district level. Legal aid is available at every stage — from the police station to the Supreme Court. It includes a lawyer, filing of court documents, and all legal representation.

## Who is Eligible for Free Legal Aid

Free legal aid is available to: (a) women — all women irrespective of income are entitled to free legal aid; (b) children — all persons below 18 years; (c) victims of trafficking or beggary; (d) persons with disabilities; (e) victims of mass disaster, ethnic violence, caste atrocity, or flood/drought; (f) industrial workmen; (g) persons in custody (arrested or detained); (h) persons with annual income below Rs. 3 lakh (or as prescribed by State Government). For POCSO and POSH cases, all victims are entitled to free legal aid without any income test.

## Article 39A — Constitutional Right to Free Legal Aid

Article 39A of the Indian Constitution (inserted by 42nd Amendment, 1976) directs the State to ensure that opportunities for securing justice are not denied to any citizen by reason of economic or other disabilities. Free legal aid is therefore not charity — it is a constitutional entitlement. The Supreme Court in Hussainara Khatoon v State of Bihar held that the right to free legal aid is part of the right to life under Article 21.

## NALSA — National Legal Services Authority

NALSA was established under the Legal Services Authorities Act 1987. It formulates policies and schemes for legal services. NALSA has a special scheme for victims of sexual crimes and POCSO cases. Toll-Free Helpline: 15100 (available nationally). NALSA provides: free legal representation at all stages; assistance in filing police complaints; support for victim compensation claims; legal awareness programs. NALSA has a special focus on women, children, and marginalized communities.

## State and District Legal Services Authorities

Every State has a State Legal Services Authority (SLSA) headed by the Chief Justice of the High Court. Every district has a District Legal Services Authority (DLSA) headed by the District Judge. DLSAs provide emergency legal services and can appoint a legal aid lawyer at short notice. For POCSO and POSH cases, DLSAs can provide: immediate legal consultation; support in filing FIR; legal aid lawyer for police station, court, and trial; assistance in claiming victim compensation.

## How to Get Free Legal Aid — Step by Step

Step 1: Call NALSA toll-free at 15100 and explain your situation. Step 2: Visit the nearest District Legal Services Authority (DLSA) — it is usually located at or near the District Court complex. Step 3: Fill a simple application form (the DLSA staff will help you fill it). Step 4: A legal aid lawyer will be assigned to you — usually within 1-2 days for urgent cases. Step 5: For POCSO and POSH cases, the lawyer is available immediately at the police station if needed. Step 6: There is no fee at any stage — all expenses are borne by the government. Step 7: If you are not satisfied with the legal aid lawyer, you can request a change.

## Legal Aid at the Police Station

Under Section 41D CrPC and Supreme Court guidelines, every arrested person has the right to meet a legal aid lawyer at the police station. In POCSO cases, a legal aid lawyer must be available to assist the child victim and their family from the moment of reporting. The police are required to inform the victim about their right to free legal aid. If the police refuse to register an FIR, a legal aid lawyer can assist in filing a complaint to the SP or a private complaint in court.

## Victim Compensation

Under Section 357A CrPC and NALSA's Victim Compensation Scheme, victims of sexual crimes including POCSO and sexual harassment can claim financial compensation. The amount is determined by the District Legal Services Authority. Compensation covers: medical treatment expenses; loss of livelihood; pain and suffering; educational rehabilitation for child victims; psychological counselling costs. Compensation can be awarded even before the trial concludes — the DLSA can grant interim compensation. No conviction is required for interim compensation.

## Legal Aid for POSH Cases

Under POSH, a woman filing a complaint has the right to seek legal assistance. NALSA and DLSAs can provide: a legal aid lawyer to assist with filing the complaint; representation in appeal proceedings; assistance in approaching the High Court if the ICC fails to act; free consultation on rights and options. Legal aid is available to both the complainant and to persons who cannot afford a private lawyer. The District Women and Child Development Officer can also refer POSH cases to legal aid services.
