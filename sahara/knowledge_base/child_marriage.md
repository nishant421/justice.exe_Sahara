# Child Marriage — Prohibition of Child Marriage Act 2006

## Overview

The Prohibition of Child Marriage Act (PCMA) 2006 prohibits the solemnisation of child marriages in India. Under this Act, a child is defined as a male below the age of 21 years or a female below the age of 18 years. Child marriages are voidable (not automatically void) at the option of the contracting child. The Act provides for Child Marriage Prohibition Officers (CMPOs) in every district. It is a cognizable and non-bailable offence to perform, conduct, or abet a child marriage.

## What is a Child Marriage

A child marriage is a marriage where either party is a child — a girl below 18 years or a boy below 21 years. This applies regardless of religion, caste, or personal law. Even if the marriage is performed under customs or personal law, PCMA provisions apply. The Supreme Court in Independent Thought v Union of India (2017) held that any sexual act with a minor wife is rape under POCSO, regardless of the marriage. Child marriage can co-exist with POCSO prosecution.

## Offences and Punishments Under PCMA

Performing, conducting, directing, or abetting a child marriage is punishable with rigorous imprisonment up to 2 years and fine up to Rs. 1 lakh. The adult male marrying a minor girl is liable to punishment. Persons who solemnise the marriage (priest, kazi, registrar) are liable. Parents or guardians who permit or fail to prevent the marriage are liable. The offence is cognizable (police can arrest without warrant) and non-bailable.

## Child Marriage is Voidable — Rights of the Child

Under Section 3 of PCMA, a child marriage is voidable — the contracting child may seek a decree of nullity from the District Court within 2 years of attaining majority (i.e., up to age 20 for a girl, up to age 23 for a boy). After the nullity decree, the court shall make provisions for maintenance and residence of the girl child. Children born of a child marriage are legitimate under Section 6. The court considers the welfare and best interests of the child at all times.

## Child Marriage Prohibition Officers (CMPOs)

The State Government appoints Child Marriage Prohibition Officers (CMPOs) in every district. CMPOs have powers to: prevent child marriages by seeking injunctions from courts; file FIRs against those attempting to solemnise child marriages; collect evidence; sensitise communities. Any person can approach the CMPO to report an impending or completed child marriage. The District Collector or Magistrate also has emergency powers to issue prohibitory orders.

## Reporting a Child Marriage

To report a child marriage or prevent one from happening: (a) Call Childline 1098 (24x7, free) — they will contact local authorities immediately; (b) Call 112 (Police Emergency); (c) Approach the Child Marriage Prohibition Officer (CMPO) at the District Collectorate; (d) File a complaint at the nearest police station; (e) Approach the Child Welfare Committee (CWC) in your district. In urgent cases where a marriage is about to take place, a court can issue an injunction within hours. NALSA provides free legal aid for victims of child marriage.

## Intersection with POCSO

When a child marriage involves sexual intercourse with a girl below 18, it also constitutes rape and sexual assault under POCSO. Both POCSO and PCMA can be applied simultaneously — they are not mutually exclusive. Law enforcement must treat any report of child marriage involving a girl below 18 also as a potential POCSO case. The child's consent to the marriage is irrelevant for POCSO purposes — the law treats all children below 18 as unable to consent to sexual acts.

## Support for Victims of Child Marriage

Victims or potential victims of child marriage can access: Childline 1098 (rescue and shelter); One Stop Centres (181) for shelter, legal aid, medical and counselling support; State Women and Child Development Department for rehabilitation; NALSA (15100) for free legal representation in court to seek nullity of marriage; District Legal Services Authority (DLSA) for emergency legal assistance; Shelter homes under Ujjawala scheme for rehabilitation.
