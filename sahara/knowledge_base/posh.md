# POSH Act 2013 — Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace (Prevention, Prohibition and Redressal)

## Overview

The Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace (Prevention, Prohibition and Redressal) Act 2013, commonly known as the POSH Act, is the primary Indian law protecting women from sexual harassment at the workplace. It is based on the Vishaka Guidelines laid down by the Supreme Court in 1997. The Act applies to all workplaces — government, private, organized, and unorganized sectors. Every employer with 10 or more employees must constitute an Internal Complaints Committee (ICC). The Act also covers domestic workers, contract workers, daily wage workers, interns, and volunteers.

## Section 2(n) — Definition of Sexual Harassment

Under Section 2(n) of the POSH Act, sexual harassment includes: (i) physical contact and advances; (ii) demand or request for sexual favours; (iii) making sexually coloured remarks; (iv) showing pornography; (v) any other unwelcome physical, verbal or non-verbal conduct of a sexual nature. Sexual harassment also includes: implied or explicit promise of preferential treatment (quid pro quo); implied or explicit threat of detrimental treatment; interference with work performance; creating an intimidating, offensive or hostile work environment. A single incident can constitute sexual harassment — repetition is not required.

## Section 2 — What is a Workplace

Under POSH, "workplace" is defined broadly to include: the office premises; any place visited during the course of employment; transportation provided by employer; employer-organised events outside office; client offices when visiting for work; work-from-home situations (extended by government circulars). The definition ensures that harassment occurring outside the traditional office is still covered.

## Section 4 — Internal Complaints Committee (ICC)

Every employer with 10 or more employees is legally required to constitute an Internal Complaints Committee (ICC). ICC composition must include: (a) a Presiding Officer — a senior woman employee committed to the cause of women; (b) minimum two employee members who are committed to the cause of women or have experience in social work or legal knowledge; (c) one external member from an NGO, association committed to women's causes, or a person familiar with sexual harassment issues. At least half of the total ICC members must be women. The ICC has all powers of a Civil Court for purposes of the inquiry. The ICC must meet regularly and keep records of all proceedings.

## Section 6 — Local Complaints Committee (LCC)

For workplaces with fewer than 10 employees, or where the complaint is against the employer, or where there is no ICC, the aggrieved woman can approach the Local Complaints Committee (LCC) at the District level. The LCC is constituted by the District Officer (District Magistrate or Collector). The LCC has the same powers as an ICC. Women in the domestic sector, daily wage workers, or those whose employers have fewer than 10 employees should approach the LCC.

## Section 9 — Filing a Complaint

An aggrieved woman may file a written complaint to the ICC or LCC within 3 months of the date of incident (or last incident in a series of incidents). The ICC or LCC may, for reasons to be recorded in writing, extend the time limit by another 3 months if satisfied that the circumstances prevented timely filing. If the aggrieved woman is unable to make a complaint due to physical or mental incapacity, a complaint may be filed by her relative, co-worker, or any person authorised by her. If the aggrieved woman is deceased, her legal heirs may file the complaint.

## Section 10 — Conciliation

Before initiating a formal inquiry, the ICC or LCC may, at the REQUEST of the aggrieved woman, attempt conciliation between the parties. Conciliation is not compulsory — it must be requested by the woman. No monetary settlement shall be a basis for conciliation — the settlement should be for non-monetary relief. If conciliation fails, the ICC/LCC shall proceed with formal inquiry. Terms of settlement must be recorded and complied with. If the respondent fails to comply, the ICC shall proceed with inquiry.

## Section 11 — Inquiry Process

The ICC or LCC shall complete inquiry within 90 days of receiving the complaint. Both the aggrieved woman and the respondent shall be given equal opportunity to be heard. Principles of natural justice must be followed at every stage. The inquiry shall be kept strictly confidential — parties, witnesses, and members cannot disclose any information. The ICC may recommend interim measures to the employer during the inquiry. The ICC may summon witnesses and examine documents.

## Section 12 — Interim Relief During Inquiry

During the pendency of the inquiry, the ICC or LCC may recommend the employer to: (a) transfer the aggrieved woman or the respondent to another branch or section; (b) grant leave to the aggrieved woman up to 3 months in addition to her normal leave entitlement; (c) restrain the respondent from reporting on the work performance of the aggrieved woman; (d) provide any other interim measures as appropriate. The employer must implement these recommendations promptly.

## Section 13 — Inquiry Report and Recommendations

After completing the inquiry, the ICC or LCC must submit a written report to the employer (or District Officer) within 10 days. If the allegations are proved, the ICC recommends: action in accordance with service rules (warning, suspension, dismissal); deduction of compensation from the respondent's salary; written apology. If the allegations are not proved, the ICC may recommend action against the complainant only if the complaint was false and made with malicious intent (mere inability to prove does not establish false complaint).

## Section 14 and 15 — Implementation and Penalties for Non-Compliance

The employer must act on ICC recommendations within 60 days. If the employer fails to act, the employer is liable to penalty. An aggrieved woman can appeal to the appropriate authority (Labour Court / High Court) within 90 days of receiving the inquiry report. Section 15 deals with determination of compensation taking into account: mental trauma, pain, suffering, emotional distress, medical expenses, loss of career opportunity.

## Section 19 — Employer Duties

Every employer has a statutory duty to: (i) provide a safe working environment; (ii) display the POSH Act provisions and ICC details at conspicuous places; (iii) organise workshops and awareness programs for employees at regular intervals; (iv) provide necessary facilities to ICC for dealing with complaints; (v) assist the aggrieved woman in seeking support services; (vi) submit annual report on the number of complaints to the District Officer.

## Section 26 — Penalties for Employer Non-Compliance

An employer who fails to constitute an ICC, or contravenes the provisions of the Act, shall be punishable with fine up to Rs. 50,000. On subsequent convictions, the fine is doubled and the business licence/registration may be cancelled. The employer cannot claim ignorance of the law as a defence. District Officers are empowered to conduct surprise inspections and impose penalties.

## How to File a POSH Complaint — Step by Step

Step 1: Document the incident — write down what happened, when, where, who was present. Step 2: Identify whether your workplace has an ICC (check with HR or notice boards). Step 3: File a written complaint to the ICC Presiding Officer within 3 months. Step 4: Request interim relief if needed (transfer, leave, or separation from the respondent). Step 5: If your workplace has fewer than 10 employees or has no ICC, approach the Local Complaints Committee (LCC) at your District Collectorate. Step 6: You can also file a complaint on the SHe-Box portal (shebox.nic.in) for government and private sector workplaces. Step 7: Legal aid is available free through NALSA (15100).

## SHe-Box Portal

SHe-Box (Sexual Harassment electronic Box) is an online complaint management portal launched by the Ministry of Women and Child Development. URL: shebox.nic.in. It allows women employees (government and private sector) to file, track, and monitor complaints of sexual harassment at the workplace. All complaints filed on SHe-Box are monitored by the Ministry and forwarded to the concerned ICC/LCC. This ensures accountability and speeds up redressal.

## When the ICC Does Not Act

If the ICC fails to act within the prescribed time, or if the employer ignores ICC recommendations, or if the inquiry is biased: (a) File a complaint with the District Officer (District Magistrate); (b) Approach the Labour Commissioner; (c) File a writ petition in the High Court; (d) NALSA or State Legal Services Authority can provide free legal representation. The POSH Act is enforceable by law — employer non-compliance is a criminal offence.
